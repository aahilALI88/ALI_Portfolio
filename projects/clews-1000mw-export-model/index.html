Botswana CLEWS Nexus & 1,000 MW Power Export Trade-Off Model

01

Problem Statement & Context

Botswana has large coal reserves and excellent solar resources, and is weighing a role as a power exporter within the Southern African Power Pool (SAPP). It is also one of the most water-scarce countries in the region, so every megawatt-hour exported carries a water cost.

Energy, water and land are usually planned in separate silos. This research builds the first CLEWS (Climate-Land-Energy-Water Systems) model for Botswana to ask an integrated question: for 1,000 MW of firm exports, which pathway, dry-cooled coal or utility-scale solar PV, earns more foreign exchange per unit of water consumed?

02

System Architecture & Methodology

A five-tool modelling chain, linked through a common CLEWS data schema, with market prices from the SAPP Day-Ahead Market and probabilistic uncertainty analysis.

  1. 01

    MAED-2: demand

    Projects domestic electricity demand so exports are assessed on top of national needs.

  2. 02

    MESSAGE-SPLAT: supply & nexus

    Least-cost optimisation of the power system, extended with water and land accounting, for coal and solar PV export scenarios.

  3. 03

    QGIS: spatial layer

    Maps solar resource, land availability, water sources and transmission to ground the scenarios in real sites.

  4. 04

    FINPLAN: financial viability

    Tests the financing, cash flow and foreign-exchange profile of each export pathway.

  5. 05

    SIMPACTS: external impacts

    Estimates environmental and health impacts and their costs for each scenario.

  6. 06

    SAPP DAM price testing

    Export revenues are evaluated against Southern African Power Pool Day-Ahead Market prices.

  7. 07

    Monte Carlo uncertainty

    Key inputs such as prices, costs and water availability are sampled to test how robust the ranking is.
