Engineering Project 1

ML-Powered Brain-Computer-Interface (BCI) Lower-Limb Exoskeleton

Thought-driven robotic gait assistance for post-stroke rehabilitation

Partner / Context

BIUST · led by Prof. Rodrigo Jamisola

  • Brain-Computer Interface
  • Machine Learning
  • Robotics
  • Embedded Control
  • Python

drag to rotate

01

Problem Statement & Context

Stroke frequently leaves survivors with lower-limb weakness or hemiparesis, and regaining independent walking depends on long, repetitive, therapist-intensive rehabilitation. In Botswana and across much of Southern Africa, specialist neuro-rehabilitation capacity is limited and concentrated in a few urban centres.

Conventional powered orthoses move the limb on a fixed schedule, so the patient's own intention to move is not part of the loop. Coupling the device to the patient's neural intent, read through a brain-computer interface, aims to make each movement an active, intention-driven therapy repetition.

02

System Architecture & Methodology

A closed-loop pipeline that turns EEG-recorded movement intent into safe, supervised joint actuation, developed with the robotics group at BIUST.

  1. 01

    EEG acquisition

    Scalp EEG is captured over the motor cortex while the patient attempts or imagines a leg movement.

  2. 02

    Signal conditioning

    Band-pass filtering, artefact handling and windowing prepare clean, time-aligned epochs for decoding.

  3. 03

    Feature extraction & ML decoding

    Motor-related EEG features feed a machine-learning classifier, implemented in Python, that distinguishes movement intent from rest.

  4. 04

    Intent-to-command mapping

    Decoded intent is translated into gait-phase commands for the exoskeleton, with confidence thresholds to suppress false triggers.

  5. 05

    Embedded joint control

    An embedded controller drives the hip/knee actuators through closed-loop position and torque control with joint-angle and torque limits.

  6. 06

    Safety & clinician oversight

    Mechanical stops, software limits and a clinician-accessible stop keep the human in charge of every session.

Key Outcomes, Metrics & Impact

Clinical focus
Post-stroke lower-limb rehabilitation
Control input
EEG-based movement intent (BCI)

BIUST robotics research group

  • Builds regional capability at the intersection of neuro-engineering, machine learning and embedded robotics.
  • Provides a platform for further clinical evaluation with rehabilitation specialists.
